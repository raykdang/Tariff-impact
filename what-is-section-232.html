What is Section 232? The steel, aluminum, car and wood tariffs

Section 232 is the part of US trade law that lets the President put a tariff on imports that the Commerce Department finds threaten national security. Today it puts 50% on steel and aluminum (25% from the UK), 25% on most cars and car parts, and 25% on upholstered wooden furniture, rising to 30% on 1 January 2027. These duties replace the 2026 country-wide duty rather than adding to it.

Section 232 in two sentences

Section 232 of the Trade Expansion Act of 1962 lets the President adjust imports, usually with a tariff, after the Commerce Department finds that they threaten US national security. Unlike the Section 301 duties, which target a country, a Section 232 duty targets a product, from almost every country at once.

Who it touches: anyone importing steel or aluminum, most copper articles, products made from them on the derivative lists, cars and car parts, and some wood products, and anyone buying those goods from an importer.

How Section 232 stacks with other tariffs

  • Ordinary duty: yes. The Section 232 rate is added to the ordinary duty for the code (for flat-rolled steel and pipe, and upholstered wooden seats, the ordinary duty is Free).
  • The 2026 country-wide duty: no. Goods that pay a Section 232 duty on steel, aluminum, copper, cars and parts or wood are exempt from the Section 301 country-wide duty of 24 July 2026 (as read on 25 November 2026: 12.5% added on goods of China, Vietnam and most other economies it covers, 10% on a named group of 17 that includes Mexico, Canada and India, and a top-up to a set total for the EU, Japan, South Korea, Switzerland and Taiwan).
  • The older China duties: yes. A Chinese product on Section 301 list 3 pays that 25% as well. Upholstered wooden seats from China pay 25% + 25% = 50% today, and 55% from 1 January 2027.
  • Two Section 232 duties on one product: where the car parts duty and the wood duty both apply, the car parts duty wins.

For the country-wide duty, see Section 301 excess capacity tariffs.

Example in dollars

Steel pipe, $10,000 of goods. Ordinary duty Free. From Vietnam: Section 232 50% = $5,000, and no 12.5% country-wide duty on top. From the UK: 25% = $2,500. From Canada: the same 50% as Vietnam; see steel and aluminum from Canada.

An upholstered wooden sofa valued at $400. Ordinary duty Free. From Vietnam: 25% = $100 today; $120 from 1 January 2027 at 30%. From China: 25% wood duty + 25% list 3 = $200 today, $220 from 1 January, unless the sofa is covered by a list 3 exclusion. See furniture from Vietnam and furniture from China.

What importers of these goods can do

  • Check the code, then the list. A product made partly of steel may or may not be a listed derivative. The list decides, not the material.
  • Compare origins. For steel and aluminum the origin barely matters (only the UK is lower). For cars, parts and wood it matters a lot: Japan, the EU, South Korea and Taiwan pay 15% in total.
  • Watch the dates. The wood rates rise on 1 January 2027; the machinery rates run until 31 December 2027. See tariff changes in January 2027.
  • Ask a broker how the value is counted. For derivative products made partly of metal, ask how the duty’s base is worked out for your code before you price. We have not worked that through for this page.

Questions

What is a Section 232 tariff?

A duty the President sets under Section 232 of the Trade Expansion Act of 1962, after a Commerce Department investigation finds that imports of a product threaten national security. It applies to the product from every country unless the proclamation says otherwise, and it comes on top of the ordinary duty.

What is the Section 232 tariff on steel and aluminum?

50% of the customs value for articles of steel or aluminum from every country except the United Kingdom, which pays 25%, since 6 April 2026. Some derivative products pay 25% (15% from the UK), and some machinery pays a total of 15%. Checked 25 November 2026.

Does Section 232 stack with other tariffs?

Not with the 2026 country-wide Section 301 duty: goods that pay a Section 232 duty are exempt from it. But it does stack with the ordinary duty, and for goods from China with the older Section 301 China duties, such as the 25% list 3 duty on upholstered wooden furniture.

Is the Section 232 wood tariff going up in 2027?

Yes, as scheduled. From 1 January 2027 the duty on upholstered wooden furniture rises from 25% to 30%, and on kitchen cabinets and vanities from 25% to 50%, except for countries that reach an agreement with the United States. The UK, the EU, Japan, South Korea and Taiwan have their own lower rates.
