Tariff on upholstered furniture from Vietnam: what it does to your margin

Upholstered wooden furniture entering the United States from Vietnam pays 25% in duty today, and the published schedule lifts that to 30% on 1 January 2027. This page shows the layers behind that figure, what it does to the cost of one armchair, and the price you would need to keep your margin.

The rate today

Duty on these goods is a stack of layers. We list each one with where we read it.

Total 25%

The total is 25% of the supplier price today, and 30% from 1 January 2027 if nothing changes. The temporary 10% import surcharge of 2026 ended on 24 July 2026, and the emergency duties of 2025 ended on 20 February 2026. Neither applies now.

Change already scheduled: the Section 232 rate on upholstered wooden furniture rises from 25% to 30% for goods entered on or after 1 January 2027. This rise was first set for 1 January 2026 and was put back a year by Proclamation 11000. The proclamation says the increase does not apply to countries that reach an agreement with the United States on the matter.

A fee also applies to each shipment: the Merchandise Processing Fee, 0.3464% of the goods' value with a minimum of $34.58 and a maximum of $670.86 per formal entry, from 1 October 2026.

We did not check whether any anti-dumping or countervailing duty applies to your product. A customs broker can tell you.

What counts as upholstered furniture here

This page is about upholstered seats with wooden frames. The Section 232 duty reaches the four tariff codes in the table, all under heading 9401.61. Your goods carry this duty, and escape Vietnam's 12.5%, only if they are classified under one of those codes.

An upholstered seat without a wooden frame, such as a chair with a metal frame, is another code. It is outside the Section 232 duty, and Vietnam's 12.5% Section 301 duty would apply to it, on top of the ordinary duty for its own code.

The code on your supplier's invoice is a starting point, not a ruling. Your customs broker decides the classification on the entry, and it is worth asking before you order: the difference between two neighbouring codes can be the whole duty.

Country matters too. The duty follows where the furniture is treated as made. Frames built in one country and upholstered in another raise an origin question that a broker should answer.

What it does to one armchair

One upholstered armchair with a wooden frame: supplier price $180, ocean freight and insurance $28, sold to retailers at $449. The prices are an example; the 25% and 30% are the rates above.

No dutyAt 25% (today)At 30% (from 1 January 2027)
Supplier price$180$180$180
Duty$0$45$54
Freight and insurance$28$28$28
Landed cost$208$253$262
Margin at $449$241 (53.7%)$196 (43.7%)$187 (41.6%)

At 25%, the duty takes $45 of margin from every chair, ten points of the price. To get it back:

  • Same dollars per chair: raise the price by $45, to $494. That is a 10% rise, not 25%.
  • Same percentage margin: the price has to reach about $546, a 22% rise.

The step to 30% on 1 January adds another $9 a chair. The Merchandise Processing Fee adds about 62 cents a chair at this value, or more on a small shipment where the $34.58 minimum applies.

The same product from other countries

The Section 232 rate on these codes is 25% from every country except five: 10% for the United Kingdom, and 15% for Japan, the European Union, South Korea and Taiwan. So moving an order from Vietnam to China, Mexico or India does not change this layer. For China, there is one more layer: upholstered seats with wooden frames under the four lines the wood duty covers (9401.61.4011, 9401.61.4031, 9401.61.6011 and 9401.61.6031) are also on the older China list 3, which adds 25% on top; the schedule says it is added to the wood duty (Harmonized Tariff Schedule, headings 9903.88.03 and 9903.88.04, US note 37(c), read 25 November 2026). Teak frames (9401.61.20) are not under the wood duty and pay differently; see furniture from China. One narrow exclusion, for sofas of set dimensions under 9401.61.6011, runs until 9 January 2027. So the same chair from China pays 50% today against Vietnam's 25%: ordinary duty Free, Section 232 25%, list 3 25%, and no 2026 country-wide duty, since wood products under Section 232 are exempt from it. The 10% duty on Chinese goods of November 2025 (heading 9903.01.24) ended with the other emergency duties on 20 February 2026 (Executive Order 14389, 91 FR 9437) and does not come on top. For how to compare the full landed cost, see China vs Vietnam vs Mexico vs India: landed cost compared.

What sellers of upholstered furniture can do

  • Time your orders around 1 January 2027. The rate depends on the date goods are entered, not the date they are ordered or shipped. Ask your forwarder for a realistic arrival and entry date before counting on the lower rate, and weigh the cost of holding bulky stock against the $9 a chair. Every January 2027 change, costed: US tariff changes in January 2027.
  • Check the code on every piece in a set. A collection can mix pieces under different codes with different duty.
  • Reprice by piece, not across the range. See whether to absorb the tariff or raise prices for how to test each one.
  • Use a surcharge line for trade customers if you expect to adjust again in January. See the tariff surcharge calculator.
  • Tell retailers early. The price increase letter templates are a start.

Questions

What is the tariff on upholstered furniture from Vietnam?

Upholstered seats with wooden frames from Vietnam pay 25% on entry to the United States: the ordinary duty is Free and the Section 232 duty on wood products is 25%. Vietnam's 12.5% Section 301 duty does not apply to these goods. The Section 232 rate is set to rise to 30% on 1 January 2027. Checked 25 November 2026.

How much does the tariff add to the cost of upholstered furniture from Vietnam?

On an armchair bought for $180, a 25% duty is $45. With $28 of freight, the landed cost rises from $208 to $253. At 30% the duty is $54 and the landed cost $262.

Does Vietnam's 12.5% tariff apply to upholstered furniture?

Not to upholstered seats with wooden frames. The Section 301 notice exempts wood products that pay the Section 232 duty. An upholstered seat without a wooden frame falls under another tariff code, outside the Section 232 duty, and Vietnam's 12.5% would apply to it.
