Tariff on auto parts from Japan: what it does to your margin

Passenger vehicle parts from Japan that are covered by the Section 232 action pay 15% in total on entry to the United States, ordinary duty included. Most other countries pay their ordinary duty plus 25%. Below: how the 15% works, one part worked through, and how Japan compares.

Rates last checked: 25 November 2026. Estimates only, not legal, customs or financial advice.

So for most covered parts from Japan, the duty is 15% of the customs value, full stop. You don't add the ordinary duty on top: it is counted inside the 15%. The base action began on 3 May 2025 for parts (the April 2025 proclamation, document 2025-05930). The temporary 10% surcharge of 2026 ended on 24 July 2026, and the emergency duties of 2025 ended on 20 February 2026.

Is your part covered? The Section 232 action reaches the parts it lists by tariff code, not every part that fits a car. We did not read that list. A part outside it is not under these headings, and would pay ordinary duty plus Japan's 2026 country-wide layer (topped up to 12.5% in total) instead. Look up your code in the Harmonized Tariff Schedule or ask a customs broker.

A fee also applies to each shipment: the Merchandise Processing Fee, 0.3464% of the goods' value with a minimum of $34.58 and a maximum of $670.86 per formal entry, from 1 October 2026.

We did not check whether any anti-dumping or countervailing duty applies to your product. A customs broker can tell you.

What it does to one part

One remanufactured alternator made in Japan: supplier price $120, freight $6, sold to repair shops at $210. The prices are an example; the 15% is the rate above, and the 25% column shows a country with no deal for comparison (its ordinary duty would come on top, so the real figure there is higher).

From Japan the duty takes $18 a unit. To get it back:

  • Same dollars per unit: price $228, an 8.6% rise.
  • Same percentage margin (40%): price $240, a 14.3% rise.

The Merchandise Processing Fee adds about 42 cents a unit at this value.

What parts importers and repair shops can do

  • Confirm the code is on the Section 232 list. It decides between 15% total and ordinary duty plus a 12.5% top-up.
  • Check origin, not brand. A Japanese brand made in a third country pays that country's rate.
  • Reprice by line. Fast movers can often carry an 8.6% rise; slow ones may not. See absorb the tariff or raise prices.
  • Show it on the invoice if your trade customers prefer a visible line. See the tariff surcharge calculator.

Questions

What is the tariff on auto parts from Japan?

Passenger vehicle parts from Japan covered by the Section 232 action pay 15% in total, ordinary duty included. Where the ordinary duty is already 15% or more, no 232 duty is added. Checked 25 November 2026.

Do I pay ordinary duty on top of the 15%?

No. For covered parts from Japan the 15% is the total: the Section 232 duty tops the ordinary duty up to 15%.

Is the tariff on auto parts from Japan lower than from China?

Yes. Covered parts from China pay ordinary duty plus 25%, and older duties on listed Chinese products may come on top. From Japan the total is 15%.
