Tariff on steel and aluminum from Canada: what 50% does to your margin

Articles of steel or aluminum from Canada pay a Section 232 duty of 50% on their full value since 6 April 2026, on top of ordinary duty. Only the United Kingdom pays less (25%). USMCA does not lower it. Below: the layers, one ton of steel worked through, and the exceptions that exist.

There is no Canadian rate of its own. Proclamation 11021 sets 50% for every country, with one exception: products of the United Kingdom made of UK-melted steel or UK-smelted aluminum pay 25%. We read no quota or lower rate for Canada. One search of the Federal Register for later proclamations on steel and aluminum (to 25 November 2026) showed no change to these rates; one search proves little.

The 2025 emergency duties on Canada ended on 20 February 2026. They no longer apply.

A fee also applies to each shipment: the Merchandise Processing Fee, 0.3464% of the goods' value with a minimum of $34.58 and a maximum of $670.86 per formal entry, from 1 October 2026.

We did not check whether any anti-dumping or countervailing duty applies to your product. A customs broker can tell you.

The first row is for derivative articles only: derivative articles made entirely of US-melted steel or US-smelted aluminum pay 10%. It is not a rate for articles of steel or aluminum themselves, such as the steel tube below: those pay the 50% in the first table. For a derivative, the mill certificate showing where the steel was melted is the evidence to ask for.

What it does to one ton of steel

One ton of steel tube from a Canadian mill: $1,100 a ton, freight $60, resold by a U.S. distributor at $1,450. The prices are an example; the 50% is the rate above for articles of steel, which steel tube is. If the tube is welded round pipe of nonalloy steel (7306.30) or seamless line pipe (7304.19), its ordinary duty is Free and 50% is the whole duty; for another code, add its ordinary duty on top.

At 50% there is nothing to absorb: the duty is nearly twice the margin. Keeping $290 a ton needs a price of $2,000, a 37.9% rise; keeping 20% needs $2,137.50. In practice the U.S. market price moves with the duty, and buyers shop between Canadian, U.S. and other mills.

The Merchandise Processing Fee adds about $3.81 a ton at this value, up to the $670.86 cap per entry.

Canada against other origins

For steel and aluminum articles the rate is 50% from Canada, Mexico, Germany, Japan, South Korea, Vietnam, India and every other country, and 25% from the United Kingdom. The European Union, Japan and South Korea get a 15% cap only on some derivative lists, not on steel or aluminum articles themselves. Russian aluminum pays 200%. For China, older duties on listed Chinese products may come on top of the 50%; we did not read which steel codes are listed. Source: Proclamation 11021, read 25 November 2026.

So for these goods switching between foreign mills changes little. What changes the answer is where the metal was melted, and whether your product is an article or a derivative on one of the lower lists.

What buyers of Canadian steel and aluminum can do

  • Ask for the melt and pour country on every mill certificate. Derivative articles made entirely of US-melted steel or US-smelted aluminum pay 10%.
  • Check whether the product is an article or a derivative, and on which annex. The rate runs from 15% total to 50% depending on it.
  • Price with an index or surcharge clause rather than fixed quotes. See the tariff surcharge calculator.
  • Get a broker's view on classification before you quote a customer.

Questions

What is the tariff on steel from Canada?

Articles of steel from Canada pay a 50% Section 232 duty on their full value since 6 April 2026, on top of ordinary duty. Canada has no lower rate of its own. Checked 25 November 2026.

Does USMCA exempt Canadian steel from the tariff?

No. The 50% applies to all countries except the United Kingdom. USMCA treatment lowers the rate for some machinery under Proclamation 11032, not for steel or aluminum articles.

What is the tariff on aluminum from Canada?

Articles of aluminum from Canada pay 50% under Section 232 since 6 April 2026, the same as steel. Canada's 10% country-wide duty does not apply to them.
