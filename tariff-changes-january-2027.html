US tariff changes in January 2027: what rises, and what it costs

Three US tariff changes are dated in the first days of 2027. On 1 January 2027 the Section 232 duty on upholstered wooden furniture goes from 25% to 30% (China's total on those seats goes from 50% to 55%), and kitchen cabinets and vanities go from 25% to 50% (the UK, the EU, Japan, South Korea and Taiwan have their own rates; see the table). After 9 January 2027, the Section 301 exclusions that let some Chinese goods skip the China duty run out unless they are extended. Below: each change with its legal source, how sure it is, and what it does to two real-size shipments.

How the layers stack in 2027

The China list 3 duty stacks on the wood duty. Proclamation 10976, which set the wood duty, says it is "in addition to any other duties", and Proclamation 11000 keeps that sentence. The tariff schedule says the same: any additional duty of that subchapter, which holds the China lists, is imposed in addition to the wood duty (US note 37(c)). So China's seats go from 25% + 25% to 30% + 25%.

The 2026 country-wide duty does not stack on it. The Section 301 duties of 24 July 2026 (12.5% for China and Vietnam, 10% for Malaysia) do not apply to wood products that pay the Section 232 duty (US note 52(f)(4)). That stays true when the wood rate rises.

Five origins keep their own headings. The United Kingdom pays 10%, and Japan, the European Union, South Korea and Taiwan 15%, under headings 9903.76.20 to 9903.76.24. These headings also cover kitchen cabinets and vanities, and the tariff schedule prints no 2027 date for them. Proclamation 11000 does not name them; its 2027 step is written "except for countries with which the United States reaches an agreement". Our reading is that these five rates stay as they are on 1 January, but no official text says so in words: check your broker's view before you count on it.

Other seats are not covered. Teak-framed seats (9401.61.20) and any line outside the four are not under the wood duty, so its rise does not touch them; see furniture from China for what they pay.

What does not change in January 2027

  • The Section 301 country-wide duties of 24 July 2026 (12.5% for China, Vietnam and most countries, 10% for Mexico (goods entered free under USMCA are exempt), India, Malaysia and others). We read no change dated January 2027.
  • Steel and aluminum: 50% for every country except the United Kingdom (25%), since 6 April 2026. The 15% caps on industrial machinery and the 25% on mobile industrial equipment run until 31 December 2027, not 2026.
  • The Merchandise Processing Fee: 0.3464%, minimum $34.58, maximum $670.86, set from 1 October 2026 for fiscal year 2027.
  • The 10% surcharge of 2026 and the 2025 emergency duties have ended and do not come back on 1 January.

Proposed, not dated: on 25 November 2026 USTR proposed Section 301 excess capacity tariffs on 14 economies. Comments are due 5 January 2027 and hearings start 25 January 2027; no tariff is in force or dated yet. If one is set, it is a separate change, and we will add it here.

What importers can do before the date

  • Check which goods can enter before 1 January. The rate follows the day the goods are entered, so a December sailing that arrives in January pays the January rate. Goods in a bonded warehouse pay the rate of the day they are withdrawn.
  • Measure set-size sofas against the exclusion, and plan entries by 9 January 2027 if they fit. Your broker must claim the exclusion on the entry.
  • Price in the step now. The tariff surcharge calculator shows what to add to an invoice, and absorb or raise prices weighs the choice.
  • Tell customers early. The price increase letter templates are a start.
  • Re-check in the first week of January. An agreement or a new proclamation can change these rates at short notice.

Questions

What US tariffs change on 1 January 2027?

As read in the official texts on 25 November 2026: the Section 232 duty on upholstered wooden furniture rises from 25% to 30%, and kitchen cabinets and vanities from 25% to 50%, both from 1 January 2027, except for countries that reach an agreement with the United States. Separately, the Section 301 exclusions for some Chinese goods, such as one group of set-size sofas, run only through 9 January 2027 unless USTR extends them.

Is the Section 232 wood tariff going to 30% in 2027?

Yes, as scheduled. Proclamation 11000 of 31 December 2025 says that from 1 January 2027 the duty on upholstered wooden furniture increases to 30 percent, except for countries with which the United States reaches an agreement. It applies to four tariff lines: 9401.61.4011, 9401.61.4031, 9401.61.6011 and 9401.61.6031. The same proclamation already moved this step back once, so a later proclamation or a trade deal could change it again.

What will upholstered furniture from China pay in 2027?

55% on the four upholstered wooden seat lines: the Section 232 wood duty at 30% plus the 25% Section 301 list 3 duty, against 50% today. Set-size sofas excluded from list 3 pay 25% until 31 December 2026, 30% from 1 to 9 January 2027, and 55% after that if the exclusion is not extended.

Does the date of shipping or the date of entry count?

The date of entry. The list 3 exclusions say so in words: they cover entries through 9 January 2027. Proclamation 11000's 30% is effective 1 January 2027, under the same clause, which applies its rates to goods entered for consumption, or withdrawn from a warehouse for consumption. Goods that leave the factory in December but enter the United States in January pay the January rate.

Does Vietnam or Malaysia furniture go up in 2027 too?

Yes, on the same four upholstered wooden seat lines: from 25% to 30% on 1 January 2027. Neither country's own Section 301 rate applies to these goods, and neither pays the China list 3 duty.
