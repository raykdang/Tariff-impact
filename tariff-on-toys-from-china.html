Tariff on toys from China: what it does to your margin

Toys from China pay 12.5% in total on entry to the United States since 24 July 2026. The ordinary duty on toys is Free, the older China duties don't reach toys today, and the 10% China duty of 2025 has ended. Below: each layer, one building-block set worked through, and what it means for a small shipment.

Are toys exempt from the 12.5%? No. The USTR notice records that an exemption for toys was asked for and refused (section III.E).

The 12.5% applies to goods entered on or after 12:01 a.m. eastern time on 24 July 2026. The temporary 10% import surcharge of 2026 ended that same day. The tariff schedule still prints the ended 10% China heading (9903.01.24) and the surcharge heading, but the orders behind them are no longer in effect.

The ten-digit lines of heading 9503.00.00 split toys by age group and carry no rate of their own. A product classified outside heading 9503, such as a game, a sporting good or a festive article, has its own code, its own ordinary duty and possibly an older China list. We did not read those: check your code in the Harmonized Tariff Schedule.

A fee also applies to each shipment: the Merchandise Processing Fee, 0.3464% of the goods' value with a minimum of $34.58 and a maximum of $670.86 per formal entry, from 1 October 2026.

We did not check whether any anti-dumping or countervailing duty applies to your product. A customs broker can tell you.

On a whole shipment

Duty is charged on the shipment's value, and the processing fee has a floor, so small importers pay more per unit than the rate suggests.

On the small shipment the fee is 6.9 cents a set; on the large one, 2.1 cents. Customs broker charges come on top and are also per entry.

China against other toy sources

The 2026 country-wide rate on toys is 12.5% from China, Vietnam, Thailand and the Philippines, and 10% from Indonesia, Malaysia, India and Mexico (Mexico's 10% does not apply to goods that enter free under USMCA). Indonesia and Malaysia each have their own list of goods exempt from that duty, which we have not read, so check that your toy is not on it. The ordinary duty on heading 9503.00.00 is Free from all of them, and China's only older list for toys is suspended.

So a move from China to Vietnam saves no duty on toys today, and a move to a 10% country saves 2.5 points: on the set above, 15 cents. See toys from Vietnam and China vs Vietnam vs Mexico vs India.

List 4B is suspended, not ended. If USTR ever lifted the suspension, its +15% (heading 9903.88.16) would come on top. Nothing we read says that is planned.

What toy importers can do

  • Check each code. Toys under heading 9503.00.00 pay 12.5% in total; a game or sporting good under another heading may pay much more.
  • Consolidate entries to spread the $34.58 minimum fee and broker charges.
  • Reprice at the next price point only where the margin needs it; see absorb the tariff or raise prices.
  • Tell your shops early. The price increase letter templates are a start.

Questions

What is the tariff on toys from China?

Toys from China pay 12.5% in total since 24 July 2026: the ordinary duty on toys, dolls and stuffed toys (heading 9503.00.00) is Free, the 2026 country-wide duty on China is 12.5%, and toys are on no older China list in force. Checked 25 November 2026.

Are toys from China still on the Section 301 lists?

Only on list 4B, and list 4B is suspended. Heading 9503.00.00 is not on lists 1, 2, 3 or 4A, and none of the 2024 increases names it. So no older Section 301 duty is charged on these toys today.

Does the 10% China tariff apply to toys?

No. The 10% China duty of November 2025 (heading 9903.01.24) ended on 20 February 2026 under Executive Order 14389, and the temporary 10% surcharge of 2026 ended on 24 July 2026. Neither comes on top of the 12.5%.

Is it cheaper to import toys from Vietnam than from China?

Not on duty. Toys under heading 9503.00.00 pay 12.5% in total from both. Indonesia, Malaysia, India and Mexico pay 10% (Mexico's does not apply to goods that enter free under USMCA). Indonesia and Malaysia each have their own list of goods exempt from that duty, which we have not read, so check that your toy is not on it.
