Tariff on electronics from China: laptops, smartphones and monitors

Laptops, smartphones and computer monitors from China pay none of the duties we read on entry to the United States today: their ordinary duty is Free, the older China lists don't reach them, and the tariff schedule exempts them from the 2026 country-wide duty of 12.5%. Two layers we did not read could still apply. Below: the codes, what is settled and what isn't, and why other electronics can pay much more.

Rates last checked: 25 November 2026. Estimates only, not legal, customs or financial advice.

Sources, all read 25 November 2026: the Harmonized Tariff Schedule of the United States, 2026 Revision 23, for the ordinary duty of each code; chapter 99, US note 20, for the China lists (none of the three codes is on lists 1, 2, 3 or 4A, and US note 31, the 2024 increases, names none of them); and US note 52(b), which says the 2026 country-wide duties "shall not apply" to the codes in its table, under heading 9903.05.86.

The same row of that exempt table also names 8471.41.01, 8471.49.00, 8471.50.01 and 8471.60.10 (other computers, and input or output units). We did not read their ordinary duty or China list.

Two older layers have ended: the 10% China duty of November 2025 (heading 9903.01.24) on 20 February 2026, and the temporary 10% surcharge of 2026 on 24 July 2026. The tariff schedule still prints both headings, but the orders behind them are no longer in effect.

What we did not read

Not checked: any Section 232 duty on semiconductors that could reach these goods, and any anti-dumping or countervailing duty. Either could add to the figures above. Before you price a shipment, confirm your code in the Harmonized Tariff Schedule or with a customs broker.

A fee also applies to each shipment: the Merchandise Processing Fee, 0.3464% of the goods' value with a minimum of $34.58 and a maximum of $670.86 per formal entry, from 1 October 2026.

Other electronics can pay much more

The exemption is by tariff code, not by the word "electronics". A charger, cable, speaker, earbuds, a television or a smart-home gadget each has its own code. If that code is not in the note 52(b) table, the 12.5% country-wide duty applies, and the code may also sit on an older China list: +25% (lists 1 to 3) or +7.5% (list 4A), or a 2024 increase of 25%, 50% or 100% on named products. We did not read those codes.

To see what is at stake, take a $420 product: the 12.5% layer alone would add $52.50 a unit, before that code's own ordinary duty and any list duty. A wrong code costs real money in either direction. Look up yours in the Harmonized Tariff Schedule.

China against other electronics sources

For these three codes, China is not at a duty disadvantage today: they are exempt from the 2026 country-wide duty from every country, and their ordinary duty is Free. Moving laptop, phone or monitor production out of China saves no duty on the layers we read. For other electronics, where China may pay a list duty that other countries don't, the answer differs by code. See China vs Vietnam vs Mexico vs India: landed cost compared.

What electronics importers can do

  • Check each code separately. A laptop and its charger can carry different duties.
  • Confirm the two unread layers with your customs broker before a large order.
  • Watch list 4B. It is suspended, not ended; if it were reinstated, laptops and monitors would pay its +15% (heading 9903.88.16).
  • Check what your supplier charges. A supplier or forwarder still adding a "tariff surcharge" for laptops, phones or monitors should say which duty it covers.

Questions

What is the tariff on laptops from China?

Of the layers we read, none applies. Laptops (8471.30.01) have Free ordinary duty, sit only on the suspended China list 4B, and are exempt from the 2026 country-wide duty (US note 52(b), heading 9903.05.86). We did not read any Section 232 duty on semiconductors or any anti-dumping duty. Checked 25 November 2026.

What is the tariff on smartphones from China?

Of the layers we read, none applies. Smartphones (8517.13.00) have Free ordinary duty, are on no Section 301 China list, and are exempt from the 2026 country-wide duty. Checked 25 November 2026.

Are computer monitors from China tariffed?

Flat-panel computer monitors (8528.52.00) have Free ordinary duty, sit only on the suspended list 4B, and are exempt from the 2026 country-wide duty, so none of the layers we read applies.

Does the 12.5% China tariff apply to all electronics?

No. The exempt table in US note 52(b) names laptops, smartphones and monitors, among others. Electronics whose code is not in that table pay the 12.5%, and may also pay an older China list duty of 25% or 7.5%. Check your own code.
