US tariff rates by country

Since 24 July 2026, goods entering the United States pay a country-wide Section 301 duty of 10% or 12.5%, depending on where they come from. For most countries it comes on top of the product's ordinary duty; for the EU, Japan, South Korea and Taiwan it is a minimum total. Every rate below was read at official sources on 25 November 2026.

How to read a rate: an example

A cotton T-shirt from Vietnam (HTS 6109.10.00) has an ordinary duty of 16.5%. Vietnam's country-wide duty is added on top: 16.5% + 12.5% = 29% of the customs value. On a shirt bought for $4, that is $1.16. A shirt of the same code from Japan pays at least 12.5% in total; since its ordinary duty is already 16.5%, it pays 16.5% alone. Both figures were read on 25 November 2026; see clothing from Vietnam and how to find your HTS code.

Goods that pay a different duty

Some products skip the country-wide duty and pay a Section 232 duty instead, whatever their origin. Read 25 November 2026:

  • Steel, aluminum and copper articles: +50% on the full customs value since 6 April 2026 (Proclamation 11021, 91 FR 18201), on top of the ordinary duty. The UK pays 25% on steel and aluminum melted or smelted there. Some derivative articles pay +25%. See steel and aluminum from Canada.
  • Auto parts: +25% on top of the ordinary duty. Parts from the EU, Japan, South Korea and Taiwan pay at least 15% in total instead, parts from the UK pay 10% instead of 25%, and parts that qualify under USMCA pay no Section 232 duty. See auto parts from Japan and from Mexico.
  • Upholstered wooden seats (four lines of 9401.61): the Section 232 wood duty, +25%, rising to 30% on 1 January 2027. See furniture from Vietnam and tariff changes in January 2027.

China has a second layer: the older Section 301 duties of 7.5% to 100% by list, still in force and continued by USTR on 7 October 2026 (91 FR 64212), on top of the 12.5%. Furniture is on list 3 (+25%); toys, laptops and monitors are only on a suspended list, and phones on none. Laptops, phones and monitors are also exempt from the 12.5%. See electronics from China.

Brazil has a second layer too: since 22 July 2026, a separate Section 301 duty of 25% on all goods of Brazil except listed exemptions (91 FR 45516; HTS 9903.05.01), on top of the 12.5% and the ordinary duty. A non-exempt good pays the ordinary duty + 12.5% + 25%. The two duties have separate exemption lists (HTS chapter 99 note 50 for the 25%, note 52 for the 12.5%), so check your code against both: a code on only one list still owes the other duty. Section 232 goods, civil aircraft, donations and informational materials are exempt from both. USTR proposed on 25 November 2026 to charge 10% on finished aircraft from Brazil and to replace the 25% on some sugar with a per-kilo duty on over-quota sugar from every country; these are proposals, not in force. Read 25 November 2026.

How US rates got here in 2026

  • Until 20 February 2026: the 2025 emergency ("reciprocal") duties. They ended that day.
  • 24 February to 24 July 2026: a temporary 10% surcharge on most imports.
  • Since 24 July 2026: the country-wide Section 301 duties in the table above.

Rates can change again by Federal Register notice within days. A new Section 301 investigation may add duties on some economies; see Section 301 excess capacity tariffs. Pages and figures that quote rates from before 24 July 2026 are out of date.

Questions

What is the US tariff rate on imports from China?

Since 24 July 2026, 12.5% on top of the ordinary duty for the product's tariff code, plus the older China Section 301 duties of 7.5% to 100% for products on their lists. Furniture on list 3, for example, pays another 25%. Checked 25 November 2026.

What is the tariff on goods from Vietnam?

12.5% on top of the ordinary duty since 24 July 2026, under Section 301. A few upholstered wooden seat lines pay the Section 232 wood duty of 25% instead. Checked 25 November 2026.

Do Mexico and Canada pay US tariffs?

Goods that enter free under USMCA are exempt from the 10% country-wide duty. Other goods pay 10% on top of the ordinary duty. Steel, aluminum and non-USMCA auto parts pay Section 232 duties instead. Checked 25 November 2026.

Is there an extra US tariff on goods from Brazil?

Yes. Since 22 July 2026, most goods of Brazil pay a separate Section 301 duty of 25% (91 FR 45516; HTS 9903.05.01), on top of the ordinary duty and the 12.5% country-wide duty, so a non-exempt good pays the ordinary duty + 37.5%. The two duties have separate exemption lists, in HTS chapter 99 note 50 (the 25%) and note 52 (the 12.5%): check your code against both. Section 232 goods, civil aircraft, donations and informational materials are exempt from both. On 25 November 2026 USTR proposed 10% on finished aircraft from Brazil and a new per-kilo duty on over-quota sugar from all countries (comments due 30 December 2026, hearing 14 January 2027); these are not in force. Checked 25 November 2026.

Is the country rate the whole tariff?

No. For most countries it comes on top of the ordinary duty for the product's tariff code. For the EU, Japan, South Korea and Taiwan it is a minimum total. Steel, aluminum, auto parts and some wood products pay Section 232 duties instead.
