Tariff on upholstered furniture from China: what it does to your margin

Sofas, armchairs and other upholstered seats with wooden frames from China pay 50% in duty on entry to the United States today: the Section 232 duty on wood products (25%) and the older China list 3 duty (25%). The Section 232 part rises to 30% on 1 January 2027, making 55%. Below: each layer, one sofa worked through, and the one sofa size that is excluded until 9 January 2027.

The total is 50% of the supplier price today, and 55% from 1 January 2027 if nothing changes. Proclamation 11000 lifts the Section 232 rate to 30% for goods entered on or after 1 January 2027, except for countries that reach an agreement with the United States on the matter. The list 3 duty is not touched by the 2024 increases (US note 31 names none of these codes), and USTR confirmed on 7 October 2026 that the older China duties remain in effect.

Two older layers do not apply: the 10% China duty of November 2025 (heading 9903.01.24) ended on 20 February 2026, and the temporary 10% surcharge of 2026 ended on 24 July 2026. The tariff schedule still prints both headings, but the orders behind them are no longer in effect.

A fee also applies to each shipment: the Merchandise Processing Fee, 0.3464% of the goods' value with a minimum of $34.58 and a maximum of $670.86 per formal entry, from 1 October 2026.

We did not check whether any anti-dumping or countervailing duty applies to your product. A customs broker can tell you.

One sofa size is excluded until 9 January 2027

USTR excluded one group of sofas from the list 3 duty: sofas under statistical line 9401.61.6011 that are 144 to 214 cm wide, 81 to 89 cm high and 81 to 163 cm deep (US note 20(vvv), item 66, heading 9903.88.69). The exclusion runs for goods entered through 9 January 2027. A sofa that fits it pays the Section 232 duty alone: 25% today, 30% from 1 January 2027, and 55% again from 10 January 2027 unless the exclusion is renewed.

Every measurement must fit. Your broker claims the exclusion on the entry; without that claim the 50% is charged.

What it does to one sofa

One three-seat sofa with a wooden frame, made in China: supplier price $320, freight $60 a unit, sold at $899. The prices are an example; the rates are the ones above, and duty is charged on the supplier price, not the freight.

On a whole container

A container of 60 of these sofas: goods value $19,200, duty at 50% $9,600, and a processing fee of $66.51 (0.3464%, inside the minimum and maximum). At 25%, from Vietnam or Malaysia or for the excluded size, the duty would be $4,800: the list 3 layer alone costs $4,800 a container.

China against other furniture sources

For upholstered seats with wooden frames, the Section 232 duty is the same 25% from China, Vietnam and Malaysia, and lower from the United Kingdom (10%) and from Japan, the European Union, South Korea and Taiwan (15%). Only China adds the 25% list 3 duty. So moving these seats from China to Vietnam or Malaysia cuts the duty from 50% to 25% today. See furniture from Vietnam, furniture from Malaysia and China vs Vietnam vs Mexico vs India.

Weigh the saving against the cost of changing supplier: samples, minimum orders, quality checks and lead time. On the sofa above it is $80 a unit.

Other furniture from China

This page is about upholstered seats with wooden frames. Other furniture from China is also on list 3 (+25%): the wooden and other furniture codes of heading 9403 (for example 9403.60.40 and 9403.60.80, wooden furniture such as dining tables, whose ordinary duty is Free) and plastic furniture (9403.70.40 and 9403.70.80). A few narrow exclusions apply, such as crib liners. Upholstered seats of teak (9401.61.20) are also Free and on list 3.

For those other codes we did not settle whether the Section 232 wood duty or the 2026 China duty of 12.5% applies on top, and we did not read the ordinary duty of most 9403 codes. Look up your code in the Harmonized Tariff Schedule, or ask your customs broker, before you price.

What furniture importers can do

  • Check the frame and the code. The frame decides the code, and the code decides whether the 50% applies.
  • Measure against the excluded sofa size while it runs, to 9 January 2027.
  • Price in the 2027 step now. The Section 232 part rises on 1 January 2027 unless an agreement changes it.
  • Get a second supplier quote from a country without the list 3 duty.
  • Tell your customers early. The price increase letter templates are a start.

Questions

What is the tariff on furniture from China?

Upholstered seats with wooden frames (subheadings 9401.61.40 and 9401.61.60) from China pay 50% today: ordinary duty Free, the Section 232 duty on wood products 25%, and the older Section 301 duty on China list 3, 25%. The Section 232 part rises to 30% on 1 January 2027, which makes 55%. Checked 25 November 2026.

Does the 12.5% China duty of 2026 apply to furniture?

Not to upholstered seats with wooden frames. Wood products that pay the Section 232 duty are exempt from the 2026 country-wide duty (USTR notice, note 52(f)(4)). The older list 3 duty still applies on top of the Section 232 duty.

Is upholstered furniture from Vietnam or Malaysia cheaper to import than from China?

On these wooden-frame seats, by 25 points of duty. Vietnam and Malaysia pay the Section 232 duty of 25% and nothing else; China pays that 25% plus the 25% list 3 duty, 50% in all.

Do the 10% China duty of 2025 and the 2026 import surcharge still apply?

No. The 10% China duty of November 2025 (heading 9903.01.24) ended on 20 February 2026 under Executive Order 14389. The temporary 10% surcharge ended on 24 July 2026. Neither comes on top today.
