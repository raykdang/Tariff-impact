Tariff on toys from Vietnam: what it does to your margin

Toys from Vietnam pay 12.5% in total on entry to the United States since 24 July 2026: the ordinary duty on toys is Free, and the country-wide duty is 12.5%. Toys asked to be exempted and were refused. Below: the layers, one plush toy worked through, and what it means for a small shipment.

Rates last checked: 25 November 2026. Estimates only, not legal, customs or financial advice.

Are toys exempt? No. The USTR notice records that an exemption for toys was asked for and refused (section III.E). The groups it does exempt (raw materials, certain farm goods, worn clothing, aircraft, art and others) don't include toys.

The 12.5% applies to goods entered on or after 12:01 a.m. eastern time on 24 July 2026. The temporary 10% surcharge of 2026 ended that same day, and the 2025 emergency duty on Vietnam ended on 20 February 2026. Neither applies now.

The ordinary duty on heading 9503.00.00 is Free. Its ten-digit lines split toys by age group and carry no rate of their own. A product classified outside heading 9503, such as a game or a sporting good, has its own code and rate, which we did not read: check yours, and add it to the 12.5%.

A fee also applies to each shipment: the Merchandise Processing Fee, 0.3464% of the goods' value with a minimum of $34.58 and a maximum of $670.86 per formal entry, from 1 October 2026.

We did not check whether any anti-dumping or countervailing duty applies to your product. A customs broker can tell you.

What it does to one plush toy

One plush toy made in Vietnam: supplier price $4.00, freight $0.40 a unit, sold to shops at $9.00. The prices are an example; the 12.5% is the rate above, and the ordinary duty on toys under heading 9503.00.00 is Free, so 12.5% is the whole duty.

To get the 50 cents back: a price of $9.50 keeps the margin in dollars (a 5.6% rise); about $10.02 keeps it as a percentage (11.4%). Toys sell at price points, so the real choice is often between holding $9 and moving to the next one.

On a whole shipment

Duty is charged on the shipment's value, and the processing fee has a floor, so small importers pay more per unit than the rate suggests.

On the small shipment the fee is 3.5 cents a toy; on the large one, 1.4 cents. Customs broker charges come on top and are also per entry. Fewer, larger entries spread both.

Vietnam against other toy sources

The 2026 country-wide rate on toys is the same 12.5% from Vietnam as from China, Thailand or the Philippines, and 10% from Indonesia, Malaysia, India or Mexico (Mexico's 10% does not apply to goods that enter free under USMCA). For China, toys under heading 9503.00.00 are on no older China list in force: their only listing, list 4B, is suspended. The 10% duty on Chinese goods of November 2025 (heading 9903.01.24) ended with the other emergency duties on 20 February 2026 (Executive Order 14389, 91 FR 9437) and does not come on top. So toys from China also pay 12.5% in total today. The ordinary duty on these toys is Free. Sources: USTR notice, 91 FR 47318, and the Harmonized Tariff Schedule, chapter 99, US note 20, read 25 November 2026.

So a move from China to Vietnam saves no duty on toys today: both pay 12.5% in total. Moving to a 10% country saves 2.5 points: on the plush toy above, 10 cents. Weigh that against quality, minimum orders and lead time. See China vs Vietnam vs Mexico vs India: landed cost compared.

What toy importers can do

  • Check each code. Toys under heading 9503.00.00 pay Free ordinary duty, so 12.5% in total; anything classified elsewhere has its own ordinary duty to add.
  • Consolidate entries to spread the $34.58 minimum fee and broker charges.
  • Reprice at the next price point only where the margin needs it; see absorb the tariff or raise prices.
  • Tell your shops early. The price increase letter templates are a start.

Questions

What is the tariff on toys from Vietnam?

Toys from Vietnam pay 12.5% in total since 24 July 2026: the ordinary duty on toys, dolls and stuffed toys (heading 9503.00.00) is Free, and the country-wide duty is 12.5%. Toys are not exempt: an exemption was asked for and refused. Checked 25 November 2026.

Are toys exempt from the Vietnam tariff?

No. The USTR notice of July 2026 records that a toy exemption was requested and refused.

Is it cheaper to import toys from Vietnam than from China?

Both pay the same 12.5% country-wide duty since 24 July 2026, and the ordinary duty on toys is Free from both. Toys (9503.00.00) are on no older China list in force: their only listing, list 4B, is suspended. The 10% duty on Chinese goods of November 2025 ended on 20 February 2026. So toys from China and from Vietnam both pay 12.5% in total today.
