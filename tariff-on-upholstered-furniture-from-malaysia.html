Tariff on upholstered furniture from Malaysia: what it does to your margin

Upholstered seats with wooden frames entering the United States from Malaysia pay 25% in duty today, and the published schedule lifts that to 30% on 1 January 2027. Malaysia's own 10% country rate does not apply to them. Below: the layers, one dining chair worked through, and what changes if the frame is not wood.

Rates last checked: 25 November 2026. Estimates only, not legal, customs or financial advice.

The total is 25% of the supplier price today, and 30% from 1 January 2027 if nothing changes. Proclamation 11000 sets that rise for goods entered on or after 1 January 2027, and says it does not apply to countries that reach an agreement with the United States on the matter. The temporary 10% import surcharge of 2026 ended on 24 July 2026, and the emergency duties of 2025 ended on 20 February 2026. Neither applies now.

A fee also applies to each shipment: the Merchandise Processing Fee, 0.3464% of the goods' value with a minimum of $34.58 and a maximum of $670.86 per formal entry, from 1 October 2026.

We did not check whether any anti-dumping or countervailing duty applies to your product. A customs broker can tell you.

Wooden frame or not: two different duties

This page is about upholstered seats with wooden frames, the codes in the table above. Sofas, armchairs and upholstered dining chairs from Malaysia are often in this group, but the frame decides it, not the name on the catalogue.

An upholstered seat with a metal frame sits under another code. It is outside the Section 232 duty, so Malaysia's 10% country-wide duty applies to it instead, on top of the ordinary duty for its own code. We did not read that ordinary duty: look up the code in the Harmonized Tariff Schedule.

Malaysia also has its own list of products exempt from the 10% (Annex II, Part E of the USTR notice). It is printed as images and we could not read it, so we can't tell you whether a given non-wood product is on it. It does not matter for wooden-frame seats, which pay the Section 232 duty either way.

Your customs broker decides the classification on the entry. Ask before you order: on two neighbouring codes the duty can differ by the whole rate.

What it does to one dining chair

One upholstered dining chair with a wooden frame: supplier price $85, ocean freight and insurance $9 a chair, sold to retailers at $219. The prices are an example; the 25% and 30% are the rates above.

At 25%, the duty takes $21.25 from every chair. To get it back:

  • Same dollars per chair: raise the price to $240.25, a 9.7% rise.
  • Same percentage margin (57.1%): the price has to reach about $268.50, a 22.6% rise.

A set of six chairs carries $127.50 of duty today and $153 from 1 January. The Merchandise Processing Fee adds about 29 cents a chair at this value, more on a small shipment where the $34.58 minimum applies.

Malaysia against other furniture sources

On wooden-frame upholstered seats the Section 232 rate is the same 25% from Malaysia, Vietnam, Indonesia, India or Mexico. Moving the order between them does not change this layer. Only five origins pay less: the United Kingdom 10%, and Japan, the European Union, South Korea and Taiwan 15%. For the full Vietnam figures see tariff on upholstered furniture from Vietnam.

The country rate does differ on furniture outside the Section 232 codes: Malaysia and Indonesia 10%, Vietnam and Thailand 12.5%, each on top of ordinary duty. For China, the older China list 3 adds 25% on upholstered wooden seats (9401.61), wooden furniture and plastic furniture codes, on top of the Section 232 duty on the wooden ones, with a few narrow exclusions until 9 January 2027 (Harmonized Tariff Schedule, chapter 99, US note 20, headings 9903.88.03 and 9903.88.04, read 25 November 2026). The 10% duty on Chinese goods of November 2025 (heading 9903.01.24) ended with the other emergency duties on 20 February 2026 (Executive Order 14389, 91 FR 9437) and does not come on top.

What furniture importers from Malaysia can do

  • Check the frame on every piece. A dining set can mix wooden-frame chairs (25%) with metal-frame stools (10% plus ordinary duty).
  • Plan entries around 1 January 2027. The rate follows the date of entry, not the order date. Ask your forwarder for a realistic entry date before counting on 25%. Every January 2027 change, costed: US tariff changes in January 2027.
  • Price the chair, not the range. A 10% price rise covers the duty in dollars on this chair; whether customers accept it is the question in absorb the tariff or raise prices.
  • Use a surcharge line for trade buyers if you expect to move again in January. See the tariff surcharge calculator.

Questions

What is the tariff on upholstered furniture from Malaysia?

Upholstered seats with wooden frames from Malaysia pay 25% on entry to the United States: ordinary duty is Free and the Section 232 duty on wood products is 25%. Malaysia's 10% country-wide duty does not apply to them. The 25% is set to rise to 30% on 1 January 2027. Checked 25 November 2026.

Does Malaysia's 10% tariff apply to furniture?

Not to upholstered seats with wooden frames, which pay the Section 232 duty instead. Furniture outside the Section 232 codes, such as a metal-frame seat, pays Malaysia's 10% on top of the ordinary duty for its code.

Is furniture from Malaysia cheaper to import than from Vietnam?

Not on wooden-frame upholstered seats: both pay 25% under Section 232. On furniture outside those codes Malaysia's country rate is 10% and Vietnam's 12.5%, each on top of ordinary duty.
