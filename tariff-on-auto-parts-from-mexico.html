Tariff on auto parts from Mexico: what USMCA is worth to your margin

For passenger vehicle parts from Mexico, one question decides the duty: does the part qualify under USMCA? If it does, it pays no Section 232 duty. If it doesn't, it pays its ordinary duty plus 25%. Below: the rule, one part worked through both ways, and what to check.

The gap between the two columns is at least 25% of the part's value. The base Section 232 action on parts began on 3 May 2025 (the April 2025 proclamation, document 2025-05930). The 2025 emergency duties on Mexico ended on 20 February 2026, and the temporary 10% surcharge of 2026 ended on 24 July 2026. Neither applies now.

Is your part covered at all? The Section 232 action reaches the parts it lists by tariff code. We did not read that list. A part outside it is not under these headings: from Mexico it would pay ordinary duty, plus Mexico's 10% country-wide duty unless it enters free under USMCA (USTR notice, 91 FR 47318).

Whole vehicles work differently: a USMCA-qualifying passenger vehicle from Mexico pays 25% on its non-US content (heading 9903.94.03). This page is about parts.

A fee also applies to each shipment: the Merchandise Processing Fee, 0.3464% of the goods' value with a minimum of $34.58 and a maximum of $670.86 per formal entry, from 1 October 2026.

One wiring harness assembled in Mexico: supplier price $40, freight $3, sold to an assembler or distributor at $70. The prices are an example; the 25% is the rate above. What decides whether a part qualifies

USMCA has its own rules of origin for auto parts: how much of the part's value must come from the United States, Mexico or Canada, and which steps must happen there. We did not read those rules for this page, and they vary by part. What matters for the duty:

  • A claim has to be made and backed. Qualifying in fact is not enough: the importer claims USMCA treatment on the entry and keeps the certification of origin that supports it.
  • Inputs from outside North America count against you. A harness built in Mexico from imported wire and connectors may or may not meet its rule. Ask the supplier for their origin analysis, not just a certificate.
  • A broker or trade lawyer should confirm it before you price on the qualifying figure. The difference is 25 points of duty.

Mexico against other origins for parts

Covered parts from Japan, the European Union, South Korea and Taiwan pay 15% in total; from the United Kingdom 10%; from China, Vietnam, India and most other countries, ordinary duty plus 25% (and for China, older duties on listed products may come on top; we did not read the lists). A qualifying Mexican part, at no Section 232 duty, beats all of them. A non-qualifying one is among the dearest. See auto parts from Japan for the 15% case.

Questions

What is the tariff on auto parts from Mexico?

Passenger vehicle parts from Mexico that qualify under USMCA pay no Section 232 duty. Parts that don't qualify pay their ordinary duty plus 25%. Neither pays Mexico's 10% country-wide duty, from which Section 232 parts are exempt. Checked 25 November 2026.

Do USMCA auto parts from Mexico pay the 25% tariff?

No. Qualifying USMCA parts pay no Section 232 duty. The importer has to claim USMCA treatment and hold the certification of origin.

Does Mexico's 10% tariff apply to auto parts?

Not to passenger vehicle parts under the Section 232 action: the USTR notice exempts them. A part outside that action pays Mexico's 10% unless it enters free under USMCA.
